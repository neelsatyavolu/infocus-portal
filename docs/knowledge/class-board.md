# Class Board

Route: `/class-board`.

Everyone signed in can open `/class-board` directly. It is not in the sidebar. It is the classroom display for the current package cycle, or for the cycle chosen in **Admin → School year → Package Cycles → Class Board cycle** (executive producers and above; **Current cycle (automatic)** follows the cycle whose Final Cut deadline hasn't passed).

Super admins and the adviser can open **Settings → PINs** and use **Class Board PIN**. The PIN is six digits. Entering it on `/class-board` opens that page only, on that browser, for 30 days. It does not sign anyone into Portal. **New PIN** replaces the old one, and devices using the old PIN have to enter the new one. Twenty wrong tries in 15 minutes pause further tries.

The PIN screen shows six boxes and a keypad. Each typed digit shows in its box, so a typo can be spotted before the sixth digit submits the PIN. Digits can also be typed on a keyboard: Backspace deletes one and Escape clears them all. While the PIN is checked the screen says **Checking…**, then **Opening the board…**; a wrong PIN shows the reason with a warning icon and clears the boxes.

## Layout

The board fills the browser window with no sidebar, top bar or assistant. On a TV or laptop it fits on one screen with no scrolling; on phones and tablets the sections stack and the page scrolls (on a tablet, Due next and Livestreams sit on the left and Calendar on the right). Text sizes follow the screen's height, so a 1080p classroom TV gets larger type than a laptop and the board keeps the same proportions; a tall, narrow window (such as a portrait iPad) is capped by its width so text still fits the columns.

- **Nameplate** (top): the 2026 wordmark (Soft White on dark, color in light mode, links to Dashboard), **Class Board** with the number of packages, the cycle number as the title, and the cycle's focus on the line under it (up to two lines on phones). On the right, over the side column: the date (shortened on phones, such as Thu, Oct 15), a live clock, and **Updated 10:42 AM** (when the board last received data).
- **Race** (left two thirds): every package as a lane.
- **Right column**, one card in this order: **Due next**, **Livestreams**, **Calendar**.

The race's column headings and the Due next heading are the same height, so their dividers form one line across the board.

## The race

**Package of the Cycle** winners always rank first, with a green-tinted lane and **Package of the Cycle** (with an award icon) as their status. With two winners, the one whose Final Cut was turned in first is #1 and the other #2; everyone else is ranked after them. A Final Cut where every member has an official grade shows **Final Cut Graded** (it wins over Final Cut Queued).

Packages are lanes in a race, ordered by how many stages are cleared. Packages that have turned in a Final Cut rank by who turned theirs in first, each in its own place, whatever their Final Cut status (pending, queued, or graded). At the same stage, work that needs revisions ranks ahead of work pending review, and both rank ahead of work not yet submitted. For example, Version 2 Needs Revisions ranks above V2 Pending Review. Among Initial Cut revision requests at the same stage, higher versions rank first: Version 2 Needs Revisions ranks above Version 1 Needs Revisions. An approved extension moves every stage deadline for that group; for example, an October 14 Initial Cut deadline with a 10 Day Extension becomes October 24. At the same stage and step, a group with more extension days has more time left, so it ranks ahead of a group with fewer or none. Packages at the same stage and step share a place only when their extension days match, and revision requests tie only when their versions also match. Seven dots match the workflow milestones: pitch, contact, A-roll/B-roll, Initial 1, Initial 2, Initial 3, final cut. The three Initial Cut milestones follow the approval chain rather than upload versions or check-in credit. When the current cut needs revisions, the board keeps its dot at the stage that requested those revisions: adviser feedback stays at Initial 2. Feedback from older uploads does not move the current cut forward, and withdrawn approvals still move the dots back. For every cycle, Initial 1 shows the regular Initial Cut deadline. Initial 3 is three calendar days before Final Cut, and Initial 2 is four calendar days before Initial 3 (seven days before Final Cut). These dates also appear in Due next. Stages 2 and 3 are display-only reminders and do not change grades or the graded Initial Cut deadline. Without a Final Cut date, those two reminders stay unset.

Each lane shows its place, the topic, and under it the members' first names and the assigned producer (AP and a first name). A filled dot is cleared, the ringed dot is the stage in progress, and the line is green through cleared stages. The column headers show each milestone's date. The **Status** column shows the same status as a Groups tile (pending review, revisions, the approval stage) as a colored tag, up to two lines long. Groups with an approved extension show an amber tag after the names, such as **10 Day Extension** (names that don't fit end in … first). The board only shows extensions for the whole group: one granted to just some members does not appear and does not change the ranking. Empty roster rows are hidden.

When a package clears a stage while the board is open, its Status column shows a green **Cleared** tag naming that stage (for example **Cleared Init 1**) for ten minutes, so the room can see what just happened. The tag compares one refresh with the next, so it does not appear when the board first opens or right after its 3-hour reload, and a stage that moves back shows nothing.

Every package is on the board at every size. On a TV the lanes share the race's height and their text shrinks as packages are added. What a lane shows follows its own height, so no line is cut in half: a tall lane gives the topic up to two lines with the names under it, a shorter lane keeps the topic to one line, and a very short lane (many packages on a small screen) puts the topic and names on one line and keeps the status to one line. The stage names over the dots size themselves to their column, so they never run into each other; on screens narrower than 1280px they alternate between two rows so they stay readable. From 768px wide (tablets) each lane is one row as on the TV: place, package, dots, status. On phones each lane's dots run across the full width under the topic and status, and the column headings stay at the top while the race scrolls.

## Nameplate focus

During **Period 1**, the nameplate shows **Class focus** and a live minutes-and-seconds countdown with **Left in class** and a progress bar. The focus suggests the next action for the nearest upcoming deadline across cycles, including the Initial Cut review stages; it shows the cycle and whether the deadline is today, tomorrow, or further away. If no deadlines remain, it suggests planning the next package steps. The countdown turns amber for the last ten minutes and disappears at the end of class.

Outside Period 1, the same place shows **Up next**: the soonest deadline's next step, with a tag such as **in 2d** (amber when it is today or tomorrow). Its stage, date and cycle are the first row of Due next, just below. With no deadlines left, the title takes the whole left side. The focus or next step uses up to two lines.

Period 1 times come from [Paly’s public bell schedule](https://www.paly.net/campus-life/daily-bell-schedule), using the subscription feed linked from that page. The board checks the feed hourly and follows its recurring periods, holiday exclusions, special schedules, moved class days, and finals in Pacific time. Master Calendar no-school overrides also hide the countdown. If the feed is unavailable, the schedule is unclear, or the day has no Period 1, the board stays usable and shows Up next instead.

## Right column

**Due next** lists the next seven stage dates still ahead, across cycles. The contact and filming stages are labeled **Proof of Contact & Brainstorming** and **A-roll & b-roll**. Each row has a countdown tag (**Today**, **in 1d**, **in 8d**; amber for today and tomorrow), the stage, then the date and cycle (for example Oct 17 · Cycle 3). In a narrow column (phones, tablets) the date and cycle sit under the stage. Cycle 1 Initial Cut Stage 2 is September 22, 2026, and Stage 3 is September 26, 2026. Future cycles automatically use the same seven-day and three-day offsets from their Final Cut deadline; changing Final Cut updates the reminders. Grading still uses the regular Initial Cut deadline. Dates already passed are left off.

**Livestreams** lists every scheduled event in the next 14 days: the day and start time, the title, the crew (or **Crew open**), the place, and public / unlisted / unconfirmed, with a tag for crew slots (**Full**, or **1 open**, **3 open** in amber, since open slots mean crew is needed) and a green **Started** tag once it has begun. Its heading counts them (for example **8 · Next 14 days**). On a TV or laptop the list gets the room the calendar doesn't need (at least one livestream) and shows each livestream that fits as a whole row; when some don't fit, the heading says how many are left off (for example **+4 more · Next 14 days**). Phones and tablets list them all.

**Calendar** lists the weekdays in the same 14 days. Each day has one tag: **Show**, **PA**, **Class**, or **No school** (dimmed; a named holiday shows its name beside the tag). Then the names, each group led by its role: anchors, PA announcers, show manager, Spirit Week filmers (brunch, lunch, and Wednesday's night rally) and editors, and **On air** packages on the publishing queue that day. Today's row is highlighted. On a TV or laptop each day is one line (long lines end in …), and the calendar keeps room for every day, sharing out any extra height. A short window (such as 1024×768) leaves the last days off whole, never cut in half, and the heading says how many (for example **+3 more · Next 14 days**). Phones and tablets show every day and every name.

## Refreshing

The board's data refreshes every 30 seconds without reloading the page, and the clock updates every second. Every 3 hours the page fully reloads, which also picks up new deployments on the production URL. If no new data arrives for two minutes (for example the network dropped), **Updated** turns into an amber **Not updated since 10:40 AM** tag. While the browser is offline the board skips refreshes rather than leaving the screen on an offline page.

If the board fails to load, it shows **The board didn't load** with **Try again** and **Back to dashboard**, and tries again on its own every 30 seconds (the screen counts down). If it keeps happening, send the reference shown on the screen to a producer. After about five minutes of failed tries it reloads the whole page, so a classroom TV recovers without anyone touching it.

When mirroring a Mac to the classroom TV, open the board in a full-screen browser. If black bars surround the entire mirrored desktop, choose the TV as the display to optimize for in macOS **System Settings → Displays**. The board can fill the browser viewport, but cannot change the mirrored display's resolution or remove bars outside that viewport. See [Apple's TV display instructions](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/mchlp1206/mac).
