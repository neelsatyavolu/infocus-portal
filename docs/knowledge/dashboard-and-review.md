# Dashboard, packages, and activity

## Public homepage

Route: `/`.

The public homepage introduces InFocus as Palo Alto High School's student-run broadcast publication, covering campus news, community stories, and live events. Under that first paragraph are **Open InFocus Portal** and, beside it, **Equipment** (equipment checkout). The next paragraph explains that InFocus Portal is the newsroom's production workspace, links to [infocusnews.tv](https://infocusnews.tv) for published work, and links to the Portal's open-source code on GitHub. Beside the introduction on wide screens (below it on phones) are two cards: **Quick links**, with **View Master Calendar** (tagged **Class members**; it needs a Portal sign-in) and **Submit an announcement** (open to anyone; InFocus reviews every request), and **Get the apps** (InFocus Portal for iPhone, InFocus for Mac, and InFocus News for iPhone, each with its download button). Below them, **Inside the newsroom** shows four cards, each with the Portal tools behind it: Story production, Editorial feedback, Broadcast planning, and Community announcements. Video reports are introduced as “packages” for visitors unfamiliar with the term. Every public page (`/`, `/privacy`, `/support`, `/security`, `/workflow`) ends with a footer linking **Privacy policy** and **Support**.

## Dashboard / Packages

Route: `/dashboard`.

- **Students** see a header with what to do next: a tag for the current stage's due date (or **All submitted**), the package topic, teammates and producer, and an **Open** button for the current stage. Below it: **My package** (a bar for each stage, with the current one highlighted: submitted stages say **Submitted**, the others show how many days are left or how late it is and the due date; then check-ins, unread producer feedback, which opens the current stage, and extension days left, which opens `/extension-requests`), a **Grade** card (estimated letter and percent for the semester, Packages, Participation, and Livestream hours; opens `/grades` or `/livestreams`), **This week** (participation points by day; opens `/grades`), and **Your activity** (their own recent actions). A student who is not on a package this cycle sees a header saying so (ask a producer to add you), then **Grade**, **This week** and **Your activity**. If the grade can't load, **Grade** and **This week** say so and the rest of the page still shows.
- **Producers** (associates and above) see **Packages**: workspaces and their projects, not the Groups review board. The header names the workspace and counts its projects; **New project** is the button in the header (or in the middle of an empty workspace). If they are on a package roster, a **My package** strip under the header shows the topic, the four stages, and their current stage with its due date and an **Open** button (or **All submitted**). One toolbar row holds a **Workspace** picker (when they can open two or more workspaces) and, when the workspace has projects, sorts them by Name, Recent, or Size and switches Grid or List view. Grid view shows a card per project with its media count and last update; List view is a table with **Project**, **Media** and **Updated** columns; **Your activity** sits in a column on the right on wide screens and below the projects on smaller ones. Producers with owner or editor access to the workspace can create projects; any producer can delete one from its card or row (this deletes all its media).

## Executive home

Executive producers, the adviser and the super admin get their own Home at `/dashboard`. The header shows the cycle, how many things need you, how many groups are behind schedule, and the next stage deadline.

**Needs you** lists everything waiting on you, overdue first, then due soonest, then whatever has waited longest. It covers groups to approve or grade (the same ones Groups marks **Needs you**), extension requests, participation changes, access requests, livestream sign-ups, and show days in the next week that are missing anchors, PA announcers or a queued package. Each row opens the page where you act on it.

Beside the list, **Class overview** counts this cycle's groups by stage, plus how many are behind schedule or have revisions requested. **This week** shows stage deadlines for the next two weeks and shows and livestreams for the next 7 days. Your projects are below, under **Projects**.

## Projects

Opening a project goes to `/projects/[id]`: folders, uploads, versions, comments. The header names where you are (the project, a folder, **Videos**, or **Recently deleted**). The small label above the title is one level up: the workspace at the top of the project, the group inside a Cycle's stage folder, otherwise the project; the full path is in the top bar. Under the title: how many assets are in that place (subfolders included) and how many folders, plus the comments on the videos on this page when there are any. In Recently deleted it says deleted assets stay there for 7 days. A **{n} processing** tag shows while videos on the page are still uploading or processing. **Upload** adds videos or images to the open folder, or to the top of the project (hover it to see where). The **⋯** button next to it holds **New folder**, **Share links…**, **Download project (Terminal)**, **Sync all turn-in dates** (executive producers, in a Final Cut folder) and **Delete all media…**; in Recently deleted it holds **Upload media** instead of **New folder** and **Delete all media…** (the trash has its own **Delete all permanently**).

Under the header is one row: select all, the status filter (All, Ready, In progress, Failed; not shown in Recently deleted), Sort (Recent, Name, Status) and Grid or List (remembered in this browser), with a count of the videos in view (**12 assets**, **2 failed** with a status filter, or **41–80 of 112** across pages; on a phone it sits just under the row). Folders come first as small tiles with their item count (**3 items**, subfolders included) and an **Empty** tag when nothing is in them. A Cycle group shows a three-part bar for its A-roll / B-roll, Initial cut and Final cut folders (filled once a stage has something in it). Drag a video onto a folder to move it. Grid view shows a card per video (thumbnail, version, length, its approval status, or Uploading / Processing / Failed while a version isn't ready, comments, who uploaded it and when); List view shows rows with version, status, people, comments, uploader and upload date (on a phone they fold under the name). If the status filter leaves no videos but there are folders, the Assets section says **No videos here match this filter** with **Show all statuses** (folders are never filtered). Selecting videos turns the row into bulk actions, which stay pinned under the top bar while you scroll: **Upload new version**, **Move into version**, **Move to…** a folder and **Delete** (in Recently deleted: **Restore** and **Delete permanently**). Esc clears the selection.

A video's **⋯** menu opens the review, picks a version, sets its status (**In review**, **Needs changes**, **Approved**, **Aired** with an air date; a version must be approved before it is marked aired; only associate producers and above can change status, and only with edit access to the workspace: with view access the status shows **View only**), uploads a new version, moves it into another package, syncs its turn-in date, downloads the original, renames or deletes it. In a Final Cut folder each video also shows its people (avatars and names), with **Add people** or **Edit**. Deleted videos go to Recently deleted for 7 days, where they can be restored or deleted permanently (from the card, the row, or the video's **⋯** menu). On a phone, folders and videos are two across, the status filter is a menu in the same row, bulk actions sit in a bar above the tab bar (**Move to…** is a folder button), and a video's menu opens from the bottom of the screen. The Assets sidebar becomes a scrolling row of chips at the top of the project (the project, every folder, Videos, Recently Deleted) with a **+** button to create a folder.

On wide screens the **Assets** panel sits to the left of the project: the project, **Videos**, a **Folders** section with its count (**No folders yet · Create one** when there are none), and **Recently Deleted**, with a **+** to create a folder. People who can see the project's share links also get a **Share links** section (click one to copy it; past the first few it says **and N more**). A **Storage** bar at the bottom shows how much of the 1 TB the project uses. The tree and its sections fold, and this browser remembers which are open.

## Review page

`/projects/[id]/review/[mediaId]` plays one video or image. Comments pin to a timestamp (or a point on an image), take replies, and can be edited, deleted and resolved. The comments panel has **Timeline** (**Pins** on an image), **General** and, for finished videos, **Transcript** tabs, and an **All / Open / Resolved** filter that this browser remembers. Comments added since your last visit to the version carry a **New** tag, and **N new** jumps to the first one. **Import comments (CSV)** adds comments from a file, and **Export comments (CSV)** in the same ⋯ menu downloads every comment and reply on the current version (a file that Import reads back). Playback speed runs 0.5× to 2× (speed and volume are remembered in this browser). Keys on a video: Space plays or pauses, ← and → jump 5 seconds, comma and period step one frame while paused, C goes to the comment box and Esc cancels an open reply or edit (on an image, only C and Esc). In the comment box, Enter sends and Shift+Enter adds a line; on a phone or tablet keyboard Enter adds a line and **Send** posts. The box shows the playhead's timecode (or the pin) beside **Send**. On a phone or tablet held upright, the player stays pinned at the top and the comment box stays docked at the bottom while the comments scroll. The header shows the version's status as a tag, a version picker beside the title (an older version gets an **Older version** tag and a **Go to vN** button), and Download, Grades and Refresh; package decisions (Submit review, Unapprove, **Approve anyway**, **Approve**) sit at its right, under a one-line approval strip that names each stage and marks the one you can decide with **· You**. For other media a **Status** menu and one next-step button take their place. On a phone the decisions sit in a card below the player, and the comment box stays above the tab bar and the keyboard. Package approval also happens here; see `groups-and-review.md`, which also covers the downloaded-range seek bar and **Transcribe**.

## Guest links

People with owner access to the workspace can share a project or one version as a guest link (`/g/[token]`) with no Portal account: **View + Comment** or **View only**, an optional passcode (4–40 characters), and an optional expiry. Links can be revoked. Guests see a **View only** or **Can comment** tag in the review header. They cannot open the transcript, download, import comments, edit, delete or resolve comments, or make review decisions. A link that is revoked, expired, or needs a passcode opens a **Protected review link** page with a passcode box.

## Activity

`/activity` used to be a media-review event feed. It now forwards to Settings, where notification preferences live. Package-cycle work is easier to follow from Groups and student cycle tabs.

## Information

`/information` is the **Cycle guide**, the student-facing cycle and approval explainer. Open it from the **Cycle guide** link in the package stage bar, after the stages. It has three sections, with jump links on smaller screens: **Stages of a Cycle** (numbered 1, 2, 3.1–3.3 and 4, matching the stage bar), **Package Pitching** (pitching rules), and **Package Approval & Feedback Process** (the approval chain, stages 1–3, and no man-on-the-street as the primary package).
