# Groups and package review

Route: `/groups`. Producer work happens here, not on student cycle tabs.

Tiles (home): topic, members, assigned AP/EP, current/pending stage, 7-segment bar: Pitch, Contact, A/B-roll, Initial 1, Initial 2, Initial 3, Final. Initial Cut bars follow current approval stages. The Final bar fills once a Final Cut is turned in (so every queued package), graded or not. When a cut needs revisions, the package stays at the stage that requested them, and reviewers stop seeing **Needs you** until the group uploads a new version. Once a Final Cut is uploaded, each executive or super admin who has not scored it sees **Pending Grade From You** next to its status (even when it says **Final Cut Queued**) until they score every member or the Final Cut is graded. Everyone else (and that grader once they have scored) sees **Grades Pending** in that spot until every member's Final Cut grade is published, then **Grades Published**. Unapproving moves the bars back to the reopened stage. Upload versions and check-in credit do not complete approval bars. Pending-review badges include whole elapsed hours since the latest submission became ready (for example, **A-roll/B-roll Pending Review for 36h**). Older media uses its upload time when readiness history is unavailable; statuses with no known timestamp omit the duration. Groups with an approved extension show its length and the time left before the extended Final Cut closes at 11:59 PM Pacific, as quiet text with an hourglass, such as **10d extension · due in 3d** (the longest grant on the group; under a day it counts hours, such as **due in 5h**). The due time drops off once the Final Cut is turned in or the extended deadline passes. An extension with no approved days on record shows **Extension**. Opening a tile goes to the pending stage. Executives, the adviser, and super admin see the groups waiting on them first, under **Needs you**. Packages assigned to you come next, under **Your groups** (associates) or **Assigned and in review** (executives, the adviser, and super admin); everything else sits under the collapsed **Other groups** (the page remembers whether you left it open). Executives, the adviser, and super admin also keep Initial Cut stage 3 and Final Cut packages in that second section (the adviser also keeps stage 2), until the Final Cut is both graded and queued, when it moves back to **Other groups**. On `/groups`, the **Cycle** menu in the toolbar switches cycles, and the page reopens on the cycle you last picked in that browser (if that cycle still exists). Inside a group (`/groups/<group>/<stage>`), the header names the cycle, topic, members, AP and EP (**unassigned** when nobody holds that role; assigning an EP leaves the AP unassigned). Under it, the stage bar shows all five stages (Pitch, Contact, A/B-roll, Initial Cut, Final Cut), each with its status word, such as APPROVED, SUBMITTED, REVISIONS, STAGE 2 or QUEUED, and a dot on the stage where work waits; on extra-wide screens a cell can also show a short date, such as **Proofs Oct 8**, **Cut Oct 10** or **Airs Oct 21**. Its top rule is full for a finished stage and half for one in progress, and Initial Cut's rule has three parts, one per approval stage. The open stage has a green tint and underline. A green dot on a stage means it changed since you last opened it in this browser, such as new proofs, a new cut or another reviewer's decision. Every stage stays clickable. On Initial Cut pages, a compact switch just under the bar moves between Stage 1 (producer review), Stage 2 (adviser review) and Stage 3 (executive sign-off), with a check on approved stages, a dot on the one in progress and a warning mark on one that was sent back. The statuses, and the sidebar's Groups count, update right after a decision on the page. **Previous** and **Next** (or the K and J keys) step through the groups in the order the Groups page last showed them, with the position, such as **4 / 12**, between them; they are hidden when the group was opened from an email or push link. J and K do nothing while you are typing or a dialog is open. The sidebar folds to icons there on large screens. On `/groups` itself, J and K move between groups (tiles, or rows in Progress) and Enter opens one. Each tile names its pending stage beside the title and shows the check-in score beside the people line. For producers who can act on that group, a **Notes** button sits at the end of the tile's status row (a small green dot means the group already has notes). It opens the same Notes field as Package Cycle; click Save to update it in both places. Assigned associates can edit notes on packages they are not members of; executives, the adviser, and super admin can edit any group’s notes.

Two views: **Tiles** (above) and **Progress**, a table grouped by assigned executive producer with each package's associate, stage track, contact count (proofs 0/3, doc, approved), and check-in score. In Progress, executives, the adviser, and super admin can click a stage to mark it complete or incomplete; changes save automatically. Done stages are green with a check. Associates see the same stages as read-only status. The header names the cycle, counts groups and how many are done, shows executives, the adviser, and super admin a **need you** tag with how many groups wait on them, and holds **Associates** (executives, the adviser, and super admin) and **Open Package Cycle** (opens `/package-progress`). On phones those two sit in the toolbar's ⋯ menu. The toolbar holds the Tiles/Progress switch, the **Cycle** menu and Refresh. In Tiles, filter chips narrow the board: **All**, **Needs you** (executives, the adviser, and super admin) and **Behind** (groups behind schedule, only when the cycle has deadlines). While a filter is on, its matches under **Other groups** show even if you left that section folded. In Progress, a **Saving…** tag shows in the header while stage changes save, and the stage buttons wait while fresh groups load. Saving a stage, approving a pitch or Refresh also updates the sidebar's Groups count. Associates get no Needs you section, chip or sidebar count.

Tile statuses, in workflow order: **Pitch Pending**, **Brainstorming**, **Brainstorm Pending Review**, **Brainstorm Needs Revisions**, **A-roll/B-roll Pending**, **A-roll/B-roll Pending Review**, **A-roll/B-roll Needs Revisions**, **Initial Cut Pending**, **Initial Cut V1 Pending Review** (or a later version), **Initial Cut Version 2 Needs Revisions**, the adviser / executive wait labels, then **Final Cut Pending**, **Final Cut Submitted**, **Final Cut Queued**, **Final Cut Graded**. For executives, the adviser, and super admin, a tile whose current step is theirs (the adviser at Stage 2; executives at Stage 3 and at Stage 1, Brainstorming, or A-roll/B-roll on packages they are assigned to) gets a green border, moves into the **Needs you** section, and counts toward **need you** in the header and the sidebar's Groups count (current cycle); tiles waiting on someone else drop their card fill, so they read quieter. There is no Needs you or Not your stage pill. Each tile also shows a schedule pill: **On track**, or **Behind by 10d** counted from the earliest stage deadline that closed at 11:59 PM Pacific without producer approval. Groups whose Final Cut is turned in drop the pill. Deadlines: Pitch, Contact and A-roll/B-roll use the cycle dates; Initial Cut Stage 1 uses the Initial Cut date; Stages 2 and 3 use the Class Board dates (7 and 3 days before Final Cut); Final Cut counts once it is turned in. The group's approved extension (the longest grant, the same days as the extension badge) moves every deadline. A cycle with no deadlines shows no schedule pill.

Executives, adviser, and super admin see every package. Associates see groups assigned to them, plus any group with no associate producer assigned. The Portal assistant can look up the same Groups list (current stage and status) for whoever is chatting. Producers can also ask which packages they produce (as the assigned producer, not as a student member). Asking about a package also surfaces stage comments (including approval notes and clip notes), uploads, proof of contact, and player comments on the cuts.

## Approval chain

Anchored to the package row, not a single file:

`DRAFT → ASSOCIATE_REVIEW → ADVISER_REVIEW → EXECUTIVE_REVIEW → APPROVED`

| Stage | Who | Artifact |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 | Assigned producer (AP or assigned EP/super-admin). If no one is assigned, no one can approve Stage 1; assign a producer on `/package-progress` first. | Initial cut |
| Stage 2 | Adviser only | Latest initial cut (the adviser decides if it needs a revision) |
| Stage 3 | Two distinct executive producers (three if marked controversial). Adviser does not count. Portal has no button to mark a package controversial. | Latest initial cut |

A stage left unreviewed for 12 hours emails its reviewers, and again every 12 hours after (see notifications.md).

`APPROVED` unlocks Final Cut. It does not mean the package is on air. Members get an email and push reminding them to upload the Final Cut, with a direct link.

**Groups Final Cut tab (producers)**: the decision bar at the top holds **Send to queue** (**Remove from queue** once queued). What airs (headline and toss; producers who can act on the package can edit the toss) sits under the video. The column beside it (below it on narrower screens) holds everything else, top to bottom: the shared **Cycle grade** note with **Publish grades** (this note is the Final Cut feedback; the tab has no separate feedback thread; the button goes away once published, and the card says when), **Package of the Cycle** voting (see package-cycles.md), then **Member grades** with the deadline / submitted / deduction summary and one short row per member: name and official grade, then quality, effort and late penalty side by side (**How grading works** is folded until you open it). Every member row lists what each executive producer gave (quality + effort = total) and names who is still pending. Students cannot see the cycle grade until a producer publishes it, and publishing emails the members. Once every member has an official grade, the Groups tile and Class Board say **Final Cut Graded** (it wins over Final Cut Queued). Uploading a replacement Final Cut (the second revision) clears the executives' earlier scores for that package. When a Final Cut is uploaded, the assigned producer and every Final Cut grader (executive producers and super admin) get an email and push asking them to grade it.

A denial keeps the package at the stage that asked for revisions. The group uploads a new version and that same stage reviews it again; it does not restart at Stage 1. A Stage 3 denial clears earlier executive approvals, so the new version needs two fresh executive sign-offs.

On the Groups Initial Cut tab, one block under the stage bar holds the approval path (**Stage 1** producer review, **Stage 2** adviser review, **Stage 3** executive sign-off, each a link with its date) and the wait line (such as **Waiting for 2 more exec approvals**), with the buttons beside them. The decisions show only on the tab for the package's current stage; another Initial tab says **This package is in Stage N** with a **Go to Initial N** link (or **Approved for Final Cut** with **Go to Final**). The buttons start with a green **Open review · vN** for the newest cut, then **Approve** and **Needs revisions** as outline buttons (on phones, Open review and Approve are pinned at the bottom). Below, the newest version is a **Current** row with **Watch here** to play it on the page (remembered per browser), then earlier versions, each with its own result (such as **Approved in Stage 2**) and **Review**. The Feedback panel shows written notes; the automatic "submitted a review" notices fold into a closed **Review activity (N)** line under them, each with **Open review**. On the Groups tab use **Needs revisions** (on the video review screen, **Submit review (needs revisions)**) to send the package back to the group, or **Approve** to sign off on the current stage. Review decisions apply to the latest cut. Approving sends the latest version to the next stage (Stage 1 to the adviser, Stage 2 to the executives). After a reviewer at any stage submits a review with needs revisions, they can still click **Approve anyway** to send that version on without waiting for a new upload. Everyone is notified the same way as a normal approval.

**Unapprove** withdraws your own sign-off and reopens that stage for review, provided you still have permission to review that stage. Withdrawing Stage 1 or Stage 2 also clears later sign-offs; an executive withdrawing Stage 3 preserves other executives' votes. Remove a package from the publishing queue before withdrawing an approval.

Once a package reaches Stage 2, associate producers are done reviewing its Initial Cut. They can still watch it, but they cannot approve it, send it back, withdraw their Stage 1 approval, post Initial Cut feedback on Groups, or add timeline comments on the video review screen. An executive who stood in as the assigned producer can still withdraw their Stage 1 approval. An associate who is a member of the package still comments as a student.

## Approve with optional feedback

Approving pitching, brainstorming, A-roll/B-roll, or Initial Cut can include a note. Skip approves with no note. The note is posted as stage feedback and included in the members' approval email. Notes saved as `[[approved]]` are **not** revision requests.

On the **Pitch** tab, a producer who can act on the group (the assigned associate, or any executive) clicks **Mark pitch complete** once the group has presented (**Unmark pitch** undoes it). Executives, the adviser, and super admin can also approve a pending pitch from its Groups tile with **Approve pitch**, which asks the same optional feedback question; unmarking still happens on the Pitch tab. On **Contact**, the button reads **Approve anyway** until all three proofs and the brainstorm doc are in. Contact shows the three proof screenshots whole (never cropped) and the doc, each opening full size in a new tab. Every stage puts its decision in the same bar at the top (pinned above the tab bar on phones) and its feedback thread on the page beside the work. Notes saved as `[[approved]]` are **not** revision requests.

## A-roll / B-roll needs changes

If a producer who can act on the group posts Brainstorming feedback without approving, it counts as a revision request: once all three proofs and the doc are in, the student Brainstorming tab shows REVISIONS and the Groups tile shows **Brainstorm Needs Revisions**. Uploading or replacing a proof after that feedback returns it to pending review. Editing the Google Doc does not, because the Portal cannot see doc edits; the producer approves or posts new feedback instead. Approval-time `[[approved]]` notes do not count. Members get the usual comment email.

If the assigned producer posts A-roll/B-roll feedback (group note or clip note), treat it as needs changes: un-approve a-roll, show REVISIONS, email students. A later student upload returns status to pending review until the producer comments again or approves. Approval-time `[[approved]]` notes do not count as revision requests. On the Groups A-roll/B-roll tab the note box and its button both read **Request changes**, and clips uploaded after the latest request carry a green **New** mark.

In the producer's A-roll/B-roll view, clips are grouped under A-roll, B-roll and Other. Clips uploaded after the latest revision request have a **NEW** badge and highlighted border, and the decision bar shows how many, such as **2 new since your request**. Older clips stay unchanged. The highlights remain when opening a clip and clear when the producer approves or posts another revision request; uploads after that new request are highlighted again.

## Comments and mail

Stage events email and push the right people (`src/server/package-review-notify.ts`):

| Event | Who hears |
|---|---|
| All three proofs and the brainstorm doc are in | Assigned producer |
| A-roll/B-roll footage uploaded | Assigned producer (at most once per group per day) |
| New Initial Cut version, or a stage approval moves it on | That stage's reviewers: assigned producer (Stage 1), adviser (Stage 2), executive producers and super admin (Stage 3) |
| Stage left unreviewed 12 hours | Same reviewers, every 12 hours (notifications.md) |
| Final Cut uploaded | Assigned producer and every Final Cut grader |
| Pitching, Brainstorming, or A-roll/B-roll approved | Members |
| Stage 1 approved, Stage 2 approved, sent back, approved for Final Cut | Members |
| Producer feedback on a stage | Members (A-roll/B-roll feedback uses the "needs changes" mail) |

Producers write stage feedback; students can read it but not post it. Group members and the package's producer can mark a note **Mark resolved** (it gets a **Resolved** tag; **Reopen** undoes it). Resolving doesn't change the stage's status.

## Associates performance

Executives, adviser, and super admin can open **Associates** from the Groups page header (on phones, the ⋯ menu). Associates and students cannot access the popup or its API. Choose a cycle and associate. **Overview** shows the weighted score, review habits and due milestones; **Feedback** shows AI feedback quality, anonymous group reviews, and the producer's recent feedback.

The overall 0–100 score uses **25% AI feedback quality, 25% group progress, 25% responsiveness, 10% review coverage, 10% group feedback, and 5% feedback coverage**. Missing components are excluded and the remaining weights rescaled. The popup labels partial scores **provisional** and shows the percentage of score weight available. No evidence is unscored, not zero. These are management indicators, not student grades or proof that a producer caused a group's results.

Review metrics count the first response to brainstorming, A-roll/B-roll, and initial cut v1 per assigned group. Unanswered work has a 48-elapsed-hour grace period, including nights and weekends. Attributed comments, submitted reviews and approvals count. Written-feedback coverage counts eligible groups, not comment volume. Verified historical reviews still count toward coverage without submission timestamps; missing timing affects only responsiveness. Unknown approvals are excluded and older upload creation times are labeled estimates. Current assignments define the groups shown, excluding groups where the associate is a student; historical reassignments are not reconstructed. New readiness and approval events are retained in the audit log even when signoffs reset.

**Group progress** is each group's completed / due milestones, averaged equally across eligible groups. Pitching, brainstorming approval, A-roll approval, initial cut and final cut use current roster completion flags. Future or undated milestones are excluded. Approved extension days move the final-cut deadline only. This measures current progress rather than reconstructing whether historical completion was on time. Student effort and later reviewers also influence it.

**AI feedback quality**: on Feedback, click **Score feedback**. Uses Gemini `gemini-3.1-flash-lite` with fixed rubric `producer-feedback-v1`, temperature 0, validated structured output and supporting verbatim excerpts. Specificity/actionability/reasoning/constructiveness are rated 0–4 and weighted 30/30/25/15 within quality. Known author/member names, emails and links are removed; no group titles, ratings or identities are sent as model metadata. Up to 12 excerpts (600 characters each) are sampled deterministically across groups/stages; sampled groups carry equal weight. Pitching and later-cut feedback can inform quality without changing first-review timing. No written feedback on eligible reviews gives quality 0; no eligible work stays unscored. The model judges written usefulness, not correctness about unseen footage; executives should inspect its explanations. Ordinary reads and manual scoring reuse the durable hash/model/rubric cache. Inngest refreshes feedback quality daily starting at midnight America/Los_Angeles, in stable producer-name order, with a durable 30-minute wait between producers. Each producer’s recorded cycles (including past cycles) are processed with a one-minute gap between cycles. The daily run forces one fresh evaluation per producer/cycle/day even when evidence is unchanged. Its day stamp prevents completed steps from being rerolled on retries. If a refresh of the same evidence fails, the last validated score and original evaluation time remain visible with an explanatory message. Failed/missing-key/rate-limited evaluations are unavailable, never zero; no fallback model silently changes the scale. At most one uncached evaluation runs per minute across instances, to stay within the free allowance. `GEMINI_API_KEY_CHAT` must be configured server-side (shared with the Portal assistant). The cache is kept per model, so earlier Groq evaluations are not reused for Gemini; click **Score feedback** or wait for the scheduled refresh to generate a new evaluation. Quality evaluations do not send mail or change grades.

The video review header names the project and folder, shows the version's status as a tag, the title and the version picker. Each version in the picker lists its status, comment count and upload age, plus a **New** tag for a version uploaded since your last visit or how many of its comments are new; a green dot on the picker means another version has something new. On an older version an **Older version** tag and a **Go to vN** button appear (decisions apply to the latest version). On package cuts the approval strip (Stage 1, Stage 2, Stage 3, with **You** on the stage you can decide) sits under the title. On the right are **Download**, **Grades** (quick grades, in Final Cut folders, for those who can grade), Refresh, then the decisions: **Submit review (needs revisions)**, **Unapprove**, **Approve anyway** and **Approve** on package cuts (a short line under them says what to do), or for other media a **Status** menu (In review, Needs changes, Approve vN, Aired…) and the next step (**Approve vN**, then **Mark as aired…**). **Grades** opens quick grades in place of the comments panel from 1024px wide (the video keeps playing), and as a sheet on smaller screens. From 1024px wide the player and the comments sit side by side and fill the screen: drag the divider between them (or focus it and use ←/→) to resize the comments, double-click it to reset, and the width is remembered. On smaller screens the version picker and the tools (a ⋯ menu on phones) sit under the player, followed by a card with the approval strip, the instruction and the decision buttons. The player holds only the video and its controls; on phones the volume slider is hidden. The seek bar shows the parts of the video already downloaded as lighter bands, and a spinner covers the picture while the player is loading after a jump or a stall. Dragging the seek bar sends only the latest position while the previous jump is still loading, so scrubbing does not queue up downloads. There is no quality menu: InFocus Drive videos (progressive MP4) play directly in the browser at their one quality, and older Bunny videos pick their quality automatically.

The comments panel has a **Transcript** tab for anyone signed in who can view the video (not guest links, not images, only finished uploads). **Transcribe vN** there transcribes the selected version with Gemini, which can take a minute or two; the tab opens on the timestamped transcript when it is ready (if you are writing a comment, the tab gets a dot and a notice instead). Click a line to jump the player there, or **Copy** it as text. Each version is transcribed once: the transcript is saved beside the video on InFocus Drive (`<name>.transcript.json`) and reused after that. New transcriptions are limited to 10 per person per hour.

**Download** (in the header from 1024px wide, under the player on smaller screens, in the ⋯ menu on phones) has the same visibility as the Transcript tab. It saves the original file of the selected version (an Initial Cut, Final Cut, or any other upload) straight from InFocus Drive.
